# Your report

I analyzed the built-in 20 Newsgroups dataset containing four categories: `alt.atheism`, `comp.graphics`, `sci.med`, and `soc.religion.christian`. The dataset contained 2,257 documents (`load_dataset_1`), with no missing or empty documents (`check_missing_2`) and no duplicates (`check_duplicates_3`). Document-length analysis (`describe_4`) showed an overall mean length of 1,992.96 characters and a median of 1,235, with several very long outliers. The distribution can be seen in [the document-length plot](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_describe_4.png).

After tokenizing all 2,257 documents (`tokenize_5`), I constructed a unigram document-term matrix (`dtm_6`) with shape 2,257 × 35,788. It contained 365,886 non-zero entries out of 80,773,516 total elements, giving 99.547% sparsity. The most frequent terms (`term_frequency_7`) were dominated by common words such as `the`, `of`, `to`, and `and`, while `edu` also appeared frequently because of newsgroup headers and email text. A small slice of the matrix was visualized in [the DTM heatmap](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_dtm_heatmap_8.png).

Feature filtering showed that high variance does not necessarily indicate strong class-discriminative value. With a variance threshold of 0.15, `variance_filter_9` retained 709 terms and removed 35,079, but the highest-variance features were still mostly common words such as `the`, `of`, and `to`. For `comp.graphics`, Pearson filtering (`pearson_filter_10`) retained 3,418 terms, and Spearman filtering (`spearman_filter_11`) retained 4,046. Spearman produced more clearly topic-related terms such as `graphics` (0.5211), `files` (0.3167), `image` (0.3086), and `3d` (0.3060). The feature-correlation heatmap (`feature_correlation_12`) also showed strong positive correlations among common high-variance terms, as shown in [the correlation plot](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_feature_correlation_12.png).

For frequent pattern mining, I applied TF-IDF filtering and the Top-K FAE algorithm to the `comp.graphics` category (`patterns_13`). The ten returned patterns were dominated by metadata terms such as `lines`, `subject`, `organization`, and `edu`, suggesting that newsgroup structural information can still dominate frequent-pattern results. I also one-hot encoded the four category labels (`binarize_labels_14`), producing a matrix of shape 2,257 × 4. These results suggest that additional preprocessing, especially remove the headers and metadata, could make the extracted patterns more meaningful.

Finally, I compared PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP for two-dimensional visualization. PCA (`reduce_dimensions_15`) explained approximately 70.3% of the variance with its first two components but did not clearly separate the four categories ([PCA plot](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_reduce_dimensions_15.png)). In contrast, t-SNE (`reduce_dimensions_16`) and especially UMAP (`reduce_dimensions_17`) produced much clearer category clusters ([t-SNE plot](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_reduce_dimensions_16.png), [UMAP plot](../plots/張秉涵_agentic-pipeline_plot_reduce_dimensions_17.png)). Cosine similarity was 0.35324 between two `comp.graphics` documents (`cosine_similarity_18`) and 0.31592 between a `comp.graphics` and an `alt.atheism` document (`cosine_similarity_19`), showing that raw CountVectorizer features still give substantial similarity across different classes because of shared high-frequency vocabulary and metadata.
